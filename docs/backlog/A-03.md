## What the backlog says

> `findPendingThreads` matches `step:started` without completion — the shape of an in-flight remote
> call — and re-issues it. After a restart the SDK worker is invoked a second time, with a new execID,
> and the original callback is orphaned.
>
> Decide and enforce, in an ADR: **(a)** at-least-once with a stable idempotency key the SDK dedupes on,
> or **(b)** replay-aware re-arming of the original execID.
>
> The SDK contract makes (a) far more palatable than it was when the caller was hand-written — dedup can
> live in the SDK, not in every customer's controller. Whichever you pick, **write it in the protocol
> spec**, because every SDK author depends on it.

## Premise status

UNVERIFIED as written, and **the load-bearing clause is contradicted by a read**. Per the skill pack's
Traps and confirmed again by F-01.2 during this session: `replayPendingThread`
(`internal/workflow/workflow.go:295`) sets `FunctionExecID: workflow.ExecID(pt.execID)` — the
**original** execID carried out of the journal by `findPendingThreads`, not a new one. The pre-crash
callback URL therefore still routes; nothing is orphaned.

*Inferred, not executed:* the real exposure is a **duplicate dispatch with two callbacks able to arrive
for one execID**, because `internal/handlers/async_function_result.go:HandlePost` (read in full) does no
pending-state, duplicate or staleness validation — it binds the path params and sends the message.

**This divergence must be settled by stage 1 before the ADR is written.** An ADR that decides between
at-least-once-with-idempotency-key and re-arming, on the basis of a premise sentence the code
contradicts, decides the wrong question.

## Route

**Two deviations from the default pipeline.**

1. `fuse-premise-verifier` — settle the execID divergence and the duplicate-callback hypothesis.
   Numbered sub-claims: (i) does replay mint a new execID? (ii) can two callbacks arrive for one
   execID and what does the second one do? Requires the A-02 and F-02 groundwork to be meaningful.
2. `fuse-protocol-spec-author` — **not the planner.** This task's output is a decision on the wire
   surface; the skill's "Protocol tasks invert the order" applies and B-01 must carry it as decision 5.
   Its `DECISIONS` block must leave nothing `TBD`.
3. `write-adr` — the backlog explicitly says "Decide and enforce, in an ADR".
4. `fuse-implementation-planner`, then `fuse-go-implementer`, then `fuse-code-reviewer` for the
   enforcement half (the callback validation).

## Done when

Derived from the task text; the backlog gives no "Done when".

- [ ] The execID divergence is settled with executed evidence and the entry re-scoped to match.
- [ ] An ADR records the choice between (a) at-least-once with a stable idempotency key and (b)

## Out of scope

- **B-02** owns implementing the HTTP transport. **C-10** owns deriving the per-node idempotency key

  the same `replayPendingThread` by a different door.
- Do not implement a transport here to "test" the decision. The spec is the deliverable.
